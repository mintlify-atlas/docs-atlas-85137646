MeetCost is software you run, not a service anyone operates for you. That means there is no processor to sign a Data Processing Agreement with — you are the data controller of everything in your deployment. This page describes what personal data MeetCost holds, how to service data subject rights using the API, and the technical and organisational measures the system provides. It is a description of what the software does, written so that your privacy counsel, DPO, or works council can assess it quickly. **It is not legal advice, and the obligations are yours.**

---

## Personal data MeetCost holds

| Data | Category | Source | Retention |
|---|---|---|---|
| Work email, name, team, manager | Personal | Your directory | Until directory sync marks the person inactive |
| Pay amount, currency, effective date | **Treat as sensitive** — equivalent to special category in practice | Your CSV upload or HRIS | Until deleted; versioned, never overwritten |
| Meeting title, times, attendee list | Personal | Your calendar system | Indefinite unless you prune |
| Attended minutes per person | **Personal and behavioural** | Provider attendance reports | Indefinite unless you prune |
| Access to pay data — who, when | Personal | Generated by the system | Indefinite by design |

<Note>
Pay is not "special category" under GDPR Article 9 in most readings, but it is among the most sensitive data an employer holds about someone. MeetCost's protections treat it that way throughout.

The row that most often surprises people is **attended minutes per person**. It is behavioural data about individuals, and it is the row a works council will ask about first.
</Note>

## Before you deploy

Work through these with whoever owns privacy at your organisation.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Establish a lawful basis">
    Most European deployments land on **legitimate interests** (Article 6(1)(f)) for cost analysis of meetings. Consent is a poor fit in an employment relationship, where it is rarely freely given. Produce a legitimate interests assessment, and ensure the balancing test engages honestly with the fact that this system can measure individuals.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assess whether a DPIA is required">
    Processing employee pay data alongside behavioural (attendance) data will usually meet the threshold for a **data protection impact assessment**. Conduct it before deployment, not after the first complaint.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Consult works council or employee representatives">
    In Germany, the Netherlands, France, and elsewhere, a system capable of monitoring employee behaviour requires consultation or co-determination before it goes live. This is not a formality. Involve representatives early — the position that MeetCost is a meeting-hygiene tool rather than a performance tool is much easier to make when representatives helped set the configuration.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Be transparent with employees">
    Employees should be told this exists, what it measures, and who can see what. A short internal note referencing your configured settings is usually enough. Deploying it quietly is the single fastest way to turn a cost-hygiene tool into a surveillance story.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check applicable national law">
    The **Ghana Data Protection Act 2012**, and similar regimes in other jurisdictions, require registration with a data protection authority and confer data subject rights on employee data on the same terms as GDPR. The API mechanisms below cover the technical side of those rights regardless of jurisdiction.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Data subject rights

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Access">
    **"What do you hold about me?"**

    Use these two endpoints to retrieve a person's pay history and a full record of who has accessed it.

    ```bash
    # Pay records, including every historical version
    curl -s "https://your-meetcost/api/v1/admin/pay?search=person@example.com&reveal=true" \
      -H "Authorization: Bearer $ADMIN_TOKEN"

    # Who has looked at this person's pay data, and when
    curl -s "https://your-meetcost/api/v1/admin/audit?target=person@example.com&days=3650" \
      -H "Authorization: Bearer $ADMIN_TOKEN"
    ```

    <Note>
    The `reveal=true` flag on the first request is itself audited — servicing an access request leaves its own trace in the log, which is the intended behaviour.
    </Note>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Erasure">
    **"Delete my pay data."**

    ```bash
    curl -X DELETE "https://your-meetcost/api/v1/admin/pay/person@example.com" \
      -H "Authorization: Bearer $ADMIN_TOKEN"
    ```

    This removes **every version** of that person's pay record. Their past meetings keep the costs already computed — those are historical financial aggregates, and recomputing or voiding them is usually neither required nor desirable under Article 17.

    If your assessment says otherwise, delete the `meeting_participants` rows directly; the meeting totals will no longer reconcile, which is the trade.

    <Warning>
    The audit log is deliberately not erasable through the API. A log of who accessed someone's salary is a protection for that person, and the retention case for it is strong. Your counsel may disagree; if so, delete directly from `audit_events` and record why.
    </Warning>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Rectification">
    **"Correct my record."**

    Upload a corrected pay record:

    - **Same `effective_date`** as an existing record → replaces that version in place.
    - **New `effective_date`** → adds a version rather than rewriting history.

    The versioning is intentional. A meeting costed last quarter should still be explainable with the rate that applied then.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Objection and restriction">
    **"Stop processing my data" / "Restrict processing."**

    There is no per-person opt-out flag. The practical mechanism is to delete the person's pay record: they are then priced at the org-wide fallback rate and labelled as an estimate, and no individual figure about them is held or processed.

    ```bash
    # Remove pay record (see Erasure tab for the full DELETE command)
    # After deletion, this person's meetings display as estimated cost
    ```
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Technical measures

| Measure | What it does |
|---|---|
| AES-256-GCM on pay amounts | Encrypted at rest under a key you supply and hold. MeetCost cannot recover your data without it and has no key-escrow mechanism |
| Ciphertext bound to the subject | Additional authenticated data ties each ciphertext to the employee's email address. A row copied between employees fails to decrypt rather than silently reporting the wrong person's salary |
| Masked by default | Pay figures are not rendered unless someone deliberately reveals them. The mask is fixed-width — an earlier variable-width version leaked the order of magnitude of a salary without going through the audited reveal path |
| Audit on every reveal | One row per person per event, so "who has seen my salary?" is always answerable. A single bulk-export row would not serve that question |
| Role-based access | Raw pay is Admin-only by default; Finance access is a separate, off-by-default flag. See [Permissions](/security/permissions) |
| Breakdown suppression and banding | Prevents a cost figure from being arithmetically reversed into a salary for non-admin viewers |
| Egress allowlist | Enforced at the socket layer. No data leaves the deployment's configured destinations. Any connection to a host not derived from your configuration is refused before a packet is sent |
| No telemetry | Nothing is reported to any vendor, including this project. Not optional-and-on-by-default. Absent |

## Organisational measures worth having

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Name someone accountable" icon="user-check">
    Name a person responsible for the deployment. "Whoever set it up" is not a role and does not constitute accountability.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Review the audit log" icon="file-magnifying-glass">
    The audit log only protects people if someone reads it. Schedule a periodic review. Use `audit?target=` for any person who asks.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Keep the admin set small" icon="users">
    Admin can read every salary and change every disclosure setting. Limit admin role assignments accordingly, and audit who holds it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Rotate service tokens" icon="rotate">
    Service tokens are admin credentials. Treat them with the same rotation schedule as other privileged credentials. Leaked tokens are leaked admin access.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Separate key and database backups" icon="key">
    Store the encryption key and the database backups in different places. Together they are the payroll file. Separately, neither is.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Produce a legitimate interests assessment" icon="scale-balanced">
    Document the balancing test before deployment, and revisit it if the configuration changes in ways that increase individual monitoring.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Data residency

The processing happens wherever you run MeetCost. There is no vendor-operated endpoint, no sub-processor, and no international transfer introduced by MeetCost itself. Directory and attendance reads go to your own tenant of Google, Microsoft, or Zoom — systems you already use, under the transfer arrangements you already have with them.

<Tip>
See [Privacy](/security/privacy) for the connection-by-connection detail of every outbound call MeetCost makes, and `server/tests/test_no_egress.py` for the test that enforces these claims on every commit.
</Tip>
